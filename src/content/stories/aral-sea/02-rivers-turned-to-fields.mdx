From the 1960s, Soviet planners diverted both rivers into canals to irrigate cotton and
other crops across the region. The fields grew; the water reaching the sea did not.

With less water coming in than evaporating, the shoreline retreated year after year.
Ports were left kilometres from the water, and the remaining sea grew saltier. By the late
1980s the Aral had separated into two parts: a smaller **North Aral** fed by the Syr Darya,
and a larger **South Aral** fed by what was left of the Amu Darya.
