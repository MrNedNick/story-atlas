Lake Chad lies where four countries meet — **Chad**, **Cameroon**, **Niger** and
**Nigeria** — on the southern edge of the Sahara. It is very shallow, only a few metres
deep in most places, so a small change in the water it receives moves its shoreline a
long way.

Most of that water comes from one river system, the **Chari**, flowing in from the south.
When the Chari is low, the lake does not just get shallower: it gets smaller, and a ridge
of vegetation across the middle can split it into a northern and a southern pool.
