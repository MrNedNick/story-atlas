The droughts that hit the Sahel in the 1970s and 1980s cut the Chari's flow, and the
lake shrank to a fraction of the area it covered in the 1960s. The northern pool dried
out in most years; the southern pool held on. Irrigation along the rivers took a share of
what was left.

The Aral Sea shrank because its rivers were diverted. Lake Chad's story is less one-sided:
rainfall did most of the work, and it can come back. Satellite studies of the last two
decades suggest the lake has held steady rather than continuing to shrink — but for the
millions of people who farm, fish and herd around its shores, a lake that breathes is
still a hard neighbour.
